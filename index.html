FAQ support agent: a showcase

A portfolio project: a chatbot that answers customer questions from a company's public help pages, cites the page it used, and says "I don't know" and offers a person when the pages do not cover the question.

These are recordings, not a live bot. This page is static and cannot run the bot. Every answer below was given by the real bot in a saved test run and is replayed exactly as recorded. Answers can be wrong; the failures are shown on purpose.

Brightwave, a made-up streaming service

Brightwave is not a real company. It is a made-up streaming service whose help pages were written only to test the bot. Nothing here is copied from a real company, and no real customer is involved.

What it "sells" in the test pages: three plans (Basic with ads at $6.99 per month, Standard at $12.99 per month or $129 per year, Premium at $18.99 per month with 4K video), 1, 2 or 4 simultaneous streams by plan, a 7-day free trial of Standard, and service in the United States, Canada and the United Kingdom. A refund is possible within 14 days of the first payment of a new subscription.

Recorded run: the real model (Sonnet 5.5), 2026-10-31, one run of 62 test questions, no query rewrite, no judge. In that run it answered 31 of 41 answerable questions correctly, declined 11 of 11 unanswerable ones, and handled 10 of 10 traps and injection attempts safely. It also declined 10 answerable questions it should have answered; two are shown below.

Missouri Department of Revenue (real, public pages)

This is NOT an official Missouri Department of Revenue bot. It is an independent learning project that was tested on the department's public help pages. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the State of Missouri. Its answers can be wrong; always check the cited page on the official site, dor.mo.gov.

What was crawled

Only the public help pages under three paths of dor.mo.gov (the FAQ pages, motor vehicle pages and driver license pages), after the project owner approved the site, the paths and the rate. The crawl followed the site's robots.txt, waited 3 seconds between requests, identified itself as a personal learning project, and stopped at a 150-request cap: 132 pages stored. The pages were crawled on 2026-11-01. They are kept only on the owner's computer and are not republished here; this page shows only the bot's own short answers, page titles and links.

The test

46 questions: 30 that the pages answer, 8 that they do not, 4 policy traps and 4 injection attempts. Recorded run (2026-11-01): query rewrite on, judge on, Sonnet 5.5, one run, no retries.

  • 17 of 30 answerable questions answered correctly (56.7%).
  • 0 wrong answers. Every miss was a decline, not a wrong fact.
  • 8 of 8 unanswerable questions declined with an offer of a person.
  • 8 of 8 traps and injection attempts handled safely.

Source and rights. Answers may quote short passages of the cited page. The State of Missouri keeps its content rights; follow the link under each answer for the source.

Caveats. The 30 answerable questions were written by this project from the same pages, so the test is not blind and is probably optimistic. One run each, and the model is not deterministic: the same code scored 16 to 17 of 30 across two runs, so one question (3.3 points) is inside the noise. The questions cover a small part of 130-plus pages. Fees and rules change, so a recorded answer may be out of date. The score is correct answers, not coverage: about 13 answerable questions were declined, and the "honest failures" below are two of them.

How it works

Find, then answer from only that

The help pages are crawled politely, cleaned, and cut into short passages, each stored with the address of its page. When a customer asks something, the bot searches those passages for the best few. Optionally a model first rewrites the question in the pages' own vocabulary ("my tags ran out" becomes "expired registration"), which found the right passage far more often in testing. The model is then given only those passages, marked as data, and is told to answer from them alone and to say when they do not contain the answer.

Every answer cites a page

The links under an answer are built by code from the passages the bot actually used, not typed by the model. No citation, no answer.

Checks after the model answers

  • Numbers: a price, fee, date or count must appear in the cited passages.
  • Links: an answer may not contain a web address the passages do not contain.
  • Promises: words like "guarantee" or "will refund" are refused unless the passage itself says so.
  • Injection echo: if page text or the customer tells the bot to ignore its rules, the bot treats that as data, and an answer that repeats such an instruction is refused.
  • Sentence grounding: each sentence must share its substance with the cited passages.

If any check fails, the customer gets a fixed message instead: "I couldn't find that in our help pages. For help from a person, please use the contact page for this site." Nothing is sent to anyone. It is written by code, never by the model. The recorded answers on this page were captured earlier and show the older wording, "Want me to pass your question to a person?", which has since been replaced.

The optional judge

A second model call can read the answer next to the cited passages and say whether each claim is supported. It can make the bot more cautious. In a narrow case it can also accept an answer the plain checks refused (for example an answer that only repeats the customer's own number, as in the recorded "four months" answer). It is off by default.

What it cannot do yet

  • Answer every question the pages cover. On Missouri it declined about 13 of the 30 answerable questions; about 6 were a retrieval miss where the right passage was not found.
  • Run live on this page. A hosted bot would call a model for every visitor, which needs an API key and per-question billing, and that is a decision for the project owner. Nothing here calls a model.
  • Prove it on questions it has never seen. The test questions were written from the same pages.
  • Cover whole sites. Only about 130 pages were stored for Missouri, and rules change after a crawl, so the stored pages need refreshing.
  • Understand images, forms or PDFs; only plain page text is used.
  • Stand in for the organisation. It is not an official support channel and answers can be wrong.
